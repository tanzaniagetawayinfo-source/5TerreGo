Vernazza Train station

Vernazza · Cinque Terre e Golfo dei Poeti

Getting off the train in Vernazza is a unique thrill. The platforms are narrow and mostly carved directly into the mountainside, with tunnels that suddenly open up to the bright colors of the village. As soon as you exit the station, you find yourself on Via Roma, the main street that leads straight down to the sea, lined with artisan shops and the irresistible scent of fresh focaccia. Inside the station you’ll find the Park Information Office where you can validate your Cinque Terre Card or ask for live updates on the trails toward Corniglia or Monterosso. The station provides restrooms (free with the Card) and, while there is no official railway luggage storage, several private "luggage storage" services are located just steps from the exit, perfect for exploring the village unburdened.

Vernazza is perhaps the village where the railway is most seamlessly integrated into local life. Just a two-minute walk from the tracks, you will find yourself in the famous harbor square, surrounded by iconic pastel houses. If you plan to depart after sunset, be careful on the platform for Track 1 (heading toward La Spezia); it is quite narrow and non-stopping trains create a powerful draft as they rush by. Always stay well behind the yellow line to safely enjoy the sight of the trains emerging from the cliff.

A look at the Station’s past:

Vernazza station stood witness to one of the most incredible events in the recent history of the Cinque Terre: the 2011 flood. On that devastating day, a river of mud and debris swept through the village filling the main street up to the level of the railway underpass. The station then became a symbol of rebirth: it was through the cleared tracks that the first aid and volunteers (the so-called "mud angels") arrived to help residents rebuild. Another curious anecdote dates back to the 19th-century construction: engineers were stunned by the hardness of Vernazza’s rock, requiring massive amounts of dynamite that shook the fishermen's houses for months. Today, that same rock shelters the station offering travelers one of the most dramatic entrances in all of Italy.
